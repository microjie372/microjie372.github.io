TTSA3R: Training-Free Temporal-Spatial Adaptive Persistent State for Streaming 3D Reconstruction

Zhijie Zheng, Xinhao Xiang, Jiawei Zhang
University of California, Davis
WACV 2027 Workshop on Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR), Oral
Paper Code arXiv


Streaming recurrent models reconstruct a scene frame by frame while keeping a fixed-size persistent state, but over long sequences they suffer from catastrophic forgetting. A uniform state update lets every new observation overwrite what the state has accumulated, which leads to geometric drift. TTSA3R replaces it with a per-token update driven by two signals the model already computes: how each state token evolves over time, and whether the image regions it attends to are changing. No additional training or forward passes are required. In the figure, CUT3R with uniform updates suffers from severe pose drift and geometric distortions (left), TTT3R improves robustness but still exhibits artifacts (middle), and our method achieves a coherent reconstruction with accurate camera poses (right).

Abstract

Streaming recurrent models enable efficient 3D reconstruction by maintaining persistent state representations. However, they suffer from catastrophic forgetting over long sequences due to balancing historical information with new observations. Recent methods alleviate this by deriving adaptive signals from the attention perspective, but they operate on single dimensions without considering temporal and spatial consistency. To this end, we propose a training-free framework termed TTSA3R that leverages both temporal state evolution and spatial observation quality for adaptive state updates in 3D reconstruction. In particular, we devise a Temporal Adaptive Update Module that regulates update magnitude by analyzing temporal state evolution patterns. Then, a Spatial Context Update Module is introduced to localize spatial regions that require updates through observation-state alignment and observation changes. These complementary signals are finally fused to determine the state updating strategies. Extensive experiments show that TTSA3R achieves competitive performance on standard short-sequence benchmarks and provides substantially stronger robustness on extended sequences. On NRGBD, as sequences extend from 50 to 250 frames, TTSA3R exhibits only a 1.33× error increase, compared with 3.8× degradation for CUT3R. This highlights the practical value of temporal-spatial adaptive updates for long-term reconstruction stability.

Framework Overview

A shared ViT encoder extracts visual tokens from the input frames, which interact with the persistent state via decoders to generate candidate states. Meanwhile, the Temporal Adaptive Update Module (TAUM) evaluates temporal state evolution across frames, and the Spatial Context Update Module (SCUM) measures spatial interaction between states and observations. Their temporal and spatial masks are combined to update the persistent state at per-token granularity, and task-specific heads predict depth maps, camera poses and pointmaps. Both signals are extracted from internal encoder and decoder representations, so TTSA3R requires no additional training or forward passes.

Temporal and Spatial Update Modules

TAUM measures how much each candidate state token changes across consecutive frames, relative to the scene average. Tokens with minimal variation have likely converged and largely retain their historical representations, while tokens with significant variation are still being refined and receive larger updates.

A token may also change substantially because of unreliable estimates, even though the image regions it attends to remain unchanged. SCUM, shown above, therefore adds spatial awareness by multiplying cross-attention between state tokens and image tokens with the cosine dissimilarity of image features across consecutive frames, so that activation is high only when both attention and divergence are substantial. The final mask is the product of the two, and the largest updates occur only when temporal dynamics and spatial correspondence are both present.

Long-Sequence Robustness

We track each task as sequences get longer, and the charts follow this order. For video depth on Bonn with metric depth accuracy, TTSA3R achieves the lowest Abs Rel and the highest δ < 1.25 at every length from 50 to 500 frames. For camera pose on TUM-dynamics and ScanNet, it achieves the lowest pose error at all sequence lengths up to 800 frames, and its margin over CUT3R widens as sequences grow. For 3D reconstruction on NRGBD, it exhibits only a 1.33× error increase as sequences extend from 50 to 250 frames, compared with 3.8× degradation for CUT3R.

Standard Benchmarks

On standard short sequences, TTSA3R remains competitive. Under per-sequence scale it achieves the best video depth among streaming methods on KITTI, and it remains competitive on the other two datasets, where StreamVGGT benefits from caching all past frames. Under metric scale it leads on Bonn. For camera pose, it achieves the lowest ATE among streaming methods on TUM-dynamics and ScanNet. On a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU, TTSA3R runs at 18.0 FPS with 6 GB of memory, comparable to TTT3R.

Effect of Each Component

Using CUT3R as the baseline, adding either module alone improves both depth and pose, and TAUM contributes the larger share, especially for pose estimation. The two modules together achieve the best performance on every metric shown.

Qualitative Results

CUT3R produces severely distorted geometry and inaccurate camera trajectories.
