PRISM: Feed-Forward Single-Image 3D Reconstruction via Geometric Warp-Residual Modeling

Zhijie Zheng, Xinhao Xiang, Jiawei Zhang
University of California, Davis
arXiv 2026
Paper Code arXiv


Camera-controlled video diffusion models achieve outstanding single-image 3D reconstruction, but iterative sampling across hundreds of video frames may take hours per scene. We observe that geometric forward warping alone can cover up to 90% of a target view when the target viewpoint stays close to the reference. PRISM therefore replaces the diffusion model at inference with a lightweight residual encoder that corrects this warped prior. (a) Given a single input image, PRISM synthesizes geometrically consistent novel views across diverse scenes. (b) Geometric forward warping covers most of a target view directly from the input. (c) PRISM achieves competitive reconstruction quality on RealEstate10K at 277× lower inference time cost than LYRA, taking 36 seconds per scene.

Abstract

Reconstructing 3D scenes from a single image is a fundamental challenge in computer vision, with broad applications in virtual reality, robotics, and content creation. Recent methods achieve outstanding performance by leveraging camera-controlled video diffusion models, but rely on iterative diffusion sampling, which greatly limits their practical deployment. We observe that geometric forward warping alone can cover the majority of a target view directly from the input image, with only a compact residual left for the encoder to correct. Motivated by this observation, we propose PRISM, a feed-forward framework that decomposes multi-view latent prediction into a parameter-free geometric prior and a learned residual correction, with no diffusion sampling required at inference. To enable generalization from purely synthetic training data, we devise a two-stage training strategy combining latents supervised distillation for geometric generalization and perceptual fine-tuning for appearance quality optimization. Extensive experiments on three benchmarks demonstrate that PRISM achieves competitive reconstruction quality compared with diffusion-based methods, while reducing inference time dramatically to only 36 seconds per scene.

From Diffusion Sampling to Geometric Reasoning

LYRA (a) relies on a frozen video diffusion model to generate multi-view latents at inference, which a 3DGS decoder turns into 3D Gaussians. PRISM (b) replaces the diffusion model with a lightweight residual encoder that predicts corrections to a parameter-free geometric prior derived from monocular depth. Video diffusion still supervises training offline, but it is not needed at inference.

Overall Framework

PRISM splits multi-view latent prediction into two parts. The geometric prior has no parameters: given monocular depth from MoGe, z-buffered forward splatting warps the reference latent to each target camera directly in the Cosmos video latent space, the representation that the supervision and the frozen decoder already use. Locations without valid warp coverage are filled with the reference latent.

The residual encoder takes the warped latent, the validity mask, the reference latent and Plücker ray embeddings, and predicts a residual correction for each view. It holds 26M parameters in 8 transformer blocks that alternate between per-view, cross-view and temporal attention, and it processes all views jointly to keep them geometrically consistent. The predicted latents are then decoded into a 3D scene by the frozen 33M 3DGS decoder adopted from LYRA, with no diffusion sampling.

Two-Stage Training

PRISM is trained on 401 synthetic scenes from the LYRA dataset, with no real-world images or multi-view captures. Target latents are generated offline with Gen3C and the Cosmos VAE encoder. Stage 1, latents supervised distillation (a), aligns intermediate features of the frozen decoder through a cosine similarity loss rather than matching raw latent values, since direct latent supervision leads to scene-specific memorization. Stage 2, perceptual fine-tuning (b), then compares rendered images through an LPIPS and MSE loss to refine appearance.

Quantitative Results

All three benchmarks are unseen during training. PRISM learns from LYRA, whose latents supervise training, so LYRA sets the ceiling these numbers are read against. Baseline numbers in Table 1 are as published, under their own protocols, so we also re-evaluated LYRA's full pipeline with our own evaluation code (Table 6). There, LYRA exceeds PRISM on all three metrics on Tanks-and-Temples. On RealEstate10K, PRISM is ahead on PSNR and essentially tied on SSIM, while LYRA remains ahead on LPIPS. PRISM's contribution is therefore inference speed, not a uniform fidelity advantage over LYRA's generative pipeline.

Inference Time

On a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU, PRISM takes 36.2 seconds per scene, against approximately 2.8 hours for Gen3C on the same GPU. The frozen 3DGS decoder accounts for 35.04 seconds, 97% of the total. PRISM's three new components (MoGe depth estimation, geometric warp and residual encoder) together contribute only 1.18 seconds. The bottleneck has shifted away from diffusion sampling.
